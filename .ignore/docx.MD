1.INTRODUCTION
1.1 BACKGROUND OF THE PROBLEM
The rapid advancement of information and communication technology has fundamentally transformed the economic landscape of societies worldwide, reshaping how goods and services are produced, promoted, and exchanged. Among the most consequential developments in this transformation is the rise of electronic commerce (e-commerce), which has evolved from a supplementary sales channel into a primary driver of economic activity across industries and geographic boundaries. Digital platforms have democratized access to markets, enabling producers of all scales to reach consumers far beyond the reach of traditional brick-and-mortar establishments (Volkova et al., 2021). Governments, non-governmental organizations, and private enterprises increasingly recognize e-commerce as a strategic tool for inclusive economic development, particularly in emerging economies where physical market infrastructure remains uneven or inaccessible (Hughes, 2025). In the Philippine context, the proliferation of smartphones, affordable mobile data, and government digital transformation initiatives has accelerated the adoption of online commerce platforms, creating unprecedented opportunities for previously marginalized sectors to participate in the digital economy (Rodrigo et al., 2024).
Despite these advancements, the benefits of digital commerce have not been equitably distributed. Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) represent one of the most persistently underserved populations in both physical and digital economic ecosystems. In the Philippines, PWDs constitute a significant portion of the population, with the Philippine Statistics Authority estimating millions of Filipinos living with some form of disability. While legislation such as Republic Act No. 7277, known as the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons, enshrines the rights of PWDs to employment, livelihood, and economic participation, the translation of these rights into practical, accessible opportunities remains deeply inadequate (Mendoza, 2023). PWDs engaged in livelihood programs frequently find that their production capabilities outpace their access to markets, resulting in a persistent gap between what they can create and what they can sell. Studies document that PWD entrepreneurs face compounding barriers including limited access to business information, absence of structured digital platforms tailored to their needs, insufficient institutional support, and low digital literacy, all of which constrain their ability to participate meaningfully in online commerce (Malhotra et al., 2025; Mendoza, 2023).
In the specific context of Region IX, the Area Vocational Rehabilitation Center (AVRC) serves as a government-operated institution under the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), mandated to provide comprehensive rehabilitation, skills training, and livelihood development services to PWDs across the region. AVRC Region IX, located in Mampang, Zamboanga City, currently serves more than sixty-six (66) PWD clients who are trained in a variety of productive livelihood activities including handweaving, handicrafts, decorative item production, recycled product creation, and other artisanal crafts. While the center's training programs have demonstrated measurable success in developing the productive skills of its clients, the absence of a structured, accessible digital channel for product promotion and sales severely limits the economic impact of these programs. At present, PWD clients rely primarily on physical selling events such as local fairs and exhibits, informal social media postings by staff, and word-of-mouth referrals, all of which are episodic, low-reach, and economically inefficient. This disconnect between skills development and market access represents a critical institutional gap that technology is uniquely positioned to address.
The development of a dedicated web-based smart e-commerce platform for AVRC Region IX offers a concrete and sustainable solution to this documented gap. Such a platform would provide PWD clients with a permanent, accessible, and organized online marketplace through which their products can be consistently promoted, discovered, and purchased by consumers across Region IX and beyond. Critically, the platform must be designed with the specific accessibility needs of PWD users at its core, incorporating features such as TalkBack voice accessibility support, high-contrast visual design, simplified navigation hierarchies, and inclusive user experience principles aligned with internationally recognized web accessibility standards (W3C, 2018; Sundaramoorthy et al., 2024). Additionally, the platform must provide Local Government Unit (LGU) staff and AVRC administrators with robust monitoring, management, and reporting tools to ensure institutional oversight, product quality control, and transparent transaction management. By bridging the gap between livelihood training and digital market access, the proposed Inclusive Market system directly operationalizes both national disability policy mandates and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 10 on Reduced Inequalities at the community level.
1.2 PROJECT CONTEXT
Inclusive Market is conceptualized within the operational environment of AVRC Region IX and the broader policy landscape of disability-inclusive economic development in the Philippines. AVRC Region IX operates under the mandate of DSWD Region IX and serves as the primary institutional home for PWD rehabilitation and livelihood training in Zamboanga Peninsula. The center's more than sixty-six (66) active PWD clients engage in regular production of handmade crafts, handwoven products, decorative items, recycled goods, souvenir items, and gift articles. Despite the variety and quality of these products, the absence of a structured digital sales channel means that market exposure remains sporadic and income generation is inconsistent.
The project positions itself at the intersection of three domains: web-based e-commerce, inclusive and accessible interface design, and government-supervised livelihood support. Inclusive Market is envisioned as a smart web-based e-commerce platform that enables PWD clients to present and sell their products through an organized digital storefront, while empowering AVRC personnel and LGU staff to monitor product listings, manage user accounts, oversee inventory, and track transactions through an administrative control panel.
The project aligns with the mandate of the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) in promoting the economic and social integration of PWDs, and with Republic Act No. 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons), which guarantees the right of PWDs to gainful employment and access to economic opportunities. It further aligns with the DSWD's community-based rehabilitation framework and Region IX's local development agenda. By creating a replicable and scalable model for digital market access within a government rehabilitation institution, Inclusive Market contributes a practical and impactful tool to the broader ecosystem of PWD empowerment and livelihood sustainability in the Philippines.
1.3 PURPOSE AND DESCRIPTION
The primary purpose of Inclusive Market is to design and develop a fully functional, accessible, and secure web-based smart e-commerce platform that bridges the gap between PWD livelihood production at AVRC Region IX and actual product commercialization in the digital marketplace. The system is designed to provide PWD clients with a user-friendly and accessible online storefront where their products can be permanently displayed, promoted, and sold, while enabling AVRC personnel and LGU staff to oversee and manage all platform operations efficiently.
Inclusive Market is described as a smart application because it incorporates intelligent organizational features, automated order processing, dynamic product promotion, and robust administrative tools that collectively enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of the e-commerce experience for all user groups. The platform prioritizes accessibility through the integration of TalkBack voice accessibility support, ensuring that PWD clients with visual or motor impairments can navigate the system independently. The interface adheres to Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) standards, featuring high-contrast design elements, enlarged interactive targets, simplified navigation structures, and clear instructional cues throughout.
The application serves three primary user groups: (1) PWD clients of AVRC Region IX who function as registered sellers and product managers, (2) AVRC personnel and LGU staff who serve as platform administrators with comprehensive monitoring and management capabilities, and (3) general consumers and buyers who browse, search, and purchase products from the marketplace. Payment processing is handled exclusively through the PayMongo Payment Gateway, ensuring secure and locally relevant transaction management. User authentication is implemented through Google GunMailer Authentication, providing a secure and accessible login experience. The system is developed as a web-based platform using modern web development technologies to ensure broad accessibility across devices without requiring software installation.
1.4 OBJECTIVES
1.4.1 GENERAL OBJECTIVES
This study aims to design and develop Inclusive Market: A Smart E-Commerce Application for Products of Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) in Area Vocational Rehabilitation Center (AVRC) Region IX — a fully accessible, web-based smart e-commerce platform that enables more than sixty-six (66) PWD clients of AVRC Region IX to promote and sell their livelihood products online, while providing AVRC personnel and LGU staff with comprehensive administrative tools for product management, inventory monitoring, order processing, and transaction oversight, ultimately advancing the economic empowerment and livelihood sustainability of persons with disabilities in Region IX.
1.4.2 SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES
1.Design and develop a smart web-based e-commerce platform for AVRC Region IX that serves as a centralized digital marketplace where PWD-produced goods such as handmade crafts, handwoven products, decorative items, recycled products, souvenir items, and gift articles can be consistently displayed, promoted, and sold to a broad consumer base.
2.Implement TalkBack voice accessibility support and inclusive interface design throughout the platform to ensure that PWD clients, including those with visual, motor, or other sensory impairments, can navigate and operate the system independently, in compliance with Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) standards.
3.Develop a comprehensive product management module that enables PWD clients and AVRC staff to add, edit, categorize, and manage product listings with images, descriptions, pricing, and availability status, ensuring that the product catalog remains accurate, organized, and visually appealing to buyers.
4.Implement inventory monitoring and order processing functionalities that allow AVRC administrators and LGU staff to track product stock levels, process customer orders, manage order statuses, and generate transaction records, providing real-time operational visibility into platform activities.
5.Integrate PayMongo Payment Gateway and Google GunMailer Authentication to provide secure, reliable, and locally accessible payment processing and user authentication services for all platform users, ensuring the protection of financial and personal data in compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act (RA 10173).
6.Develop an LGU administrative control and monitoring dashboard that enables authorized government personnel and AVRC staff to manage user accounts, approve product listings, monitor sales performance, generate reports, and oversee all marketplace transactions, ensuring institutional accountability and transparent governance of the platform.
7.Evaluate the usability, accessibility, and overall effectiveness of the developed system through structured user testing involving PWD clients and AVRC personnel, using validated evaluation instruments and usability metrics to assess system performance, user satisfaction, and accessibility compliance within the project timeline.
1.5 SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
1.5.1 SCOPE
The study encompasses the design, development, testing, and evaluation of Inclusive Market, a smart web-based e-commerce application dedicated to the promotion and sale of products produced by PWD clients of AVRC Region IX. The platform covers the following functional domains: user registration and authentication via Google GunMailer, product catalog management, product browsing and searching, shopping cart functionality, order placement and processing, secure checkout via PayMongo Payment Gateway, product promotion and marketing features, inventory monitoring, LGU and AVRC staff administrative control, user management, transaction reporting, and TalkBack voice accessibility support. The system is designed for web-based deployment and is accessible through standard web browsers on desktop and laptop computers, as well as mobile browsers, without requiring dedicated application installation. The primary users of the system include more than sixty-six (66) PWD clients of AVRC Region IX as registered sellers, AVRC personnel and LGU staff as administrators, and general consumers as buyers. The geographical scope is limited to AVRC Region IX, located in Mampang, Zamboanga City. The development and evaluation of the system will be conducted by a team of six (6) project members within the academic timeframe of the study.
1.5.2 LIMITATIONS
The study is subject to several defined limitations. The system covers only PWD-produced products from AVRC Region IX and does not extend to products from other AVRC branches, organizations, or regions. The platform requires internet connectivity for all operations and is not designed for offline access. The study does not include the development of a dedicated mobile application for Android or iOS platforms; access on mobile devices is limited to responsive web browser access. The system does not incorporate Artificial Intelligence features, machine learning algorithms, predictive analytics, chatbot functionality, or any AI-driven automation. Payment processing is limited to the PayMongo Payment Gateway and does not encompass other payment systems. Advanced logistics features such as real-time courier tracking or third-party delivery service integration are outside the scope of the study. The evaluation of the system is limited to the specified testing period and selected respondents from AVRC Region IX and will not represent large-scale commercial deployment conditions. Security features are limited to standard authentication and data protection measures implemented during development, in compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act. The platform is intended for academic and institutional purposes and may require additional enhancements for full-scale commercial deployment.

2.REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND SYSTEMS
2.1 TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
The development of Inclusive Market draws upon a broad technical foundation encompassing web-based e-commerce systems architecture, inclusive and accessible interface design, payment gateway integration, authentication systems, and digital commerce principles for community-based enterprises. A thorough understanding of these technical underpinnings is essential to appreciating the practical value and design rationale of the proposed system.
Web-based e-commerce platforms operate on a multi-tier client-server architecture that separates presentation, application logic, and data management into distinct layers. This separation of concerns enables scalability, maintainability, and the efficient handling of concurrent user sessions, which is particularly important for platforms serving users with varying device capabilities and connectivity conditions (Costa & Rodrigues, 2024). Modern web development frameworks such as Laravel, Django, or Node.js/Express provide robust foundations for building such architectures, offering built-in support for routing, authentication, database abstraction, and API development. The frontend layer, responsible for user interface rendering and interaction, is typically developed using HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript frameworks that support responsive design — ensuring consistent functionality across desktop and mobile browsers without dedicated application installation.
Accessibility in web applications represents both a technical requirement and an ethical imperative for platforms designed to serve users with disabilities. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), maintained by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), establish internationally recognized standards for web accessibility across four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust (W3C, 2018). WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance requires, among other provisions, sufficient color contrast ratios, keyboard navigability, text alternatives for non-text content, and compatibility with assistive technologies. TalkBack, a screen reader originally developed for Android devices but extending its principles to web accessibility through ARIA (Accessible Rich Internet Applications) standards, enables users with visual or motor impairments to navigate web content through audio feedback. Implementing ARIA labels, semantic HTML elements, and logical focus management throughout the Inclusive Market interface ensures compatibility with TalkBack and similar assistive technologies (Sundaramoorthy et al., 2024).
Payment gateway integration is a critical technical component of any operational e-commerce platform. PayMongo, a Philippine-based payment processing company, provides a developer-friendly API that supports a range of local payment methods including GCash, Maya, credit cards, and over-the-counter banking. Its API documentation specifies RESTful endpoints for payment intent creation, payment method attachment, and webhook-based payment status updates, enabling seamless integration into web applications developed in any major backend framework (PayMongo, n.d.). Authentication security is addressed through Google GunMailer Authentication, which leverages Google's OAuth 2.0 protocol to provide token-based, email-verified user authentication, reducing reliance on password-based systems that may be challenging for PWD users to manage independently.
From a data management perspective, relational database management systems such as MySQL or PostgreSQL provide the structured data storage required for product catalog management, user account administration, order processing, inventory tracking, and transaction recording. These systems support the complex relational queries required by administrative dashboards and reporting modules, enabling AVRC staff and LGU personnel to generate accurate, real-time operational reports. The combination of these technical components — layered web architecture, WCAG-compliant accessible design, PayMongo payment integration, Google GunMailer authentication, and relational database management — forms the comprehensive technical foundation upon which Inclusive Market is designed and developed.
2.2 RELATED STUDIES
2.2.1 FOREIGN
Study 1. Sundaramoorthy, Raleigh, and Owolabi (2024) investigated AI-driven accessible e-commerce platforms for people with visual, hearing, and mobility impairments. The study's objective was to examine how technology improves accessibility and user experience in digital commerce environments. The study employed a review-based and system-level analysis of accessibility tools including voice-assisted navigation, adaptive user interfaces, conversational assistance, sign language integration, and product discovery enhancements. Findings demonstrated that technology-enabled accessibility features promote digital inclusion and enable users with disabilities to interact more effectively with e-commerce platforms, particularly through features that reduce reliance on precise motor input and complex visual interpretation. This study is highly relevant to Inclusive Market because the proposed platform is similarly designed to create a digitally accessible marketplace for PWDs, with TalkBack voice accessibility support, high-contrast interface elements, and simplified navigation hierarchies as core design features. However, the study's primary limitation lies in its treatment of PWDs exclusively as platform customers rather than as product creators, sellers, and livelihood entrepreneurs, a gap that Inclusive Market directly addresses through its seller-centric, PWD-empowerment design philosophy.
Study 2. Gutterman (2025) analyzed market opportunities within the disability community through market analysis, policy review, and examination of disability-inclusive business practices. The study found that the disability community represents a significant but consistently underserved market segment, and that businesses and institutions stand to generate meaningful social and economic impact by designing accessible platforms that engage PWDs as consumers, suppliers, and entrepreneurs. The study introduces the concept of DisabilityTech as an emerging field focused on products and services specifically addressing the needs and opportunities of the disability market. This is directly relevant to Inclusive Market because it provides authoritative market-level justification for the creation of a dedicated digital platform for PWD-produced goods. The study strengthens the argument that PWDs should be recognized as productive economic agents rather than welfare recipients. Its limitation, however, is a broad conceptual orientation without proposing a deployable technological solution, a gap addressed by Inclusive Market through its concrete, institution-specific platform development.
Study 3. Dakung et al. (2025) explored the role of technology in entrepreneurship education and economic empowerment for persons with disabilities through a systematic review of global best practices and recent scholarship on disability-inclusive entrepreneurship. The study emphasizes that technology must be embedded in entrepreneurship support programs rather than treated as an add-on to improve accessibility and economic outcomes. This supports Inclusive Market's foundational premise that a structured digital marketplace integrated with AVRC's institutional support functions will yield greater economic participation than training alone. The study's limitation is its focus on entrepreneurship education rather than actual product commercialization, a transition that Inclusive Market operationalizes through its fully functional marketplace.

The study recommends structured institutional support and simplified tools as enablers of disabled entrepreneurship. While Inclusive Market does not incorporate AI-based recommendation engines, the study's findings on product discoverability and structured product categorization inform the platform's product browsing, search, and promotion design, ensuring that PWD-made goods are presented in organized, findable ways. The limitation is the focus on large commercial platforms rather than small community-based sellers, an orientation that Inclusive Market counters through its localized, institution-specific design.

Findings demonstrated significant positive effects of user-centered platform design on engagement metrics, with particular benefits for platforms that prioritize relevance, ease of use, and transparent information presentation. This informs Inclusive Market's UX design philosophy, which emphasizes clear product presentation, organized browsing categories, and simplified checkout processes to encourage repeat buyer engagement with PWD-produced goods. The study's limitation is its commercial e-commerce orientation without addressing social-impact or community-based marketplace contexts, which Inclusive Market bridges.

(2025) examined workforce development and institutional readiness in the context of digital economic transformation through policy analysis, finding that communities require localized, institution-embedded support systems to enable workers and producers to benefit from digital tools. The study recommends proactive institutional engagement in digital tool development and deployment rather than passive technology adoption. This supports AVRC's active role in co-managing Inclusive Market alongside PWD clients, ensuring that the platform serves as both a marketplace and an institutional enabler of digital economic participation. Findings showed that digital platforms are becoming essential tools for economic participation, business transformation, and market integration across all scales of production, including small and community-based producers. The study documents a clear global trajectory toward digital commerce as the primary channel for connecting producers and consumers, validating the strategic importance of developing accessible digital storefronts for marginalized producer groups. The limitation is the macroeconomic and country-level focus without addressing localized inclusive platforms, which Inclusive Market specifically introduces.

Findings showed that ease of use, simplified product management, and reliable transaction processing are the most critical factors determining whether small sellers successfully integrate e-commerce into their commercial activities. This directly informs Inclusive Market's design priorities, validating the emphasis on simplified product listing interfaces, clear navigation structures, and reliable PayMongo-integrated checkout processes. The limitation is the SME orientation without consideration of accessibility requirements or PWD sellers, both of which are central to Inclusive Market's design.
Study 15. Rodrigo et al. (2024) examined consumer readiness for technology integration in online shopping among Filipino consumers through quantitative PLS-SEM analysis. Findings revealed that perceived ease of use, trust, and system performance significantly influence consumer adoption of digital commerce platforms, particularly among first-time or infrequent online buyers. This is relevant to Inclusive Market because many of its target buyers in Region IX may be unfamiliar with purchasing from PWD-focused online platforms. The study's findings validate the importance of prioritizing usability, building consumer trust through transparent product information, and ensuring consistent system performance. The limitation is that the study does not develop or test an actual platform and does not address accessibility for users with disabilities, gaps that Inclusive Market directly addresses.

2.3.3 SYNTHESIS OF THE STUDY
The reviewed literature and systems collectively establish a robust foundation of evidence demonstrating the critical importance of accessible, institution-embedded digital platforms in advancing the economic participation of persons with disabilities. Across both foreign and local studies, recurring themes include the persistent barriers faced by PWDs in accessing structured digital markets (Mendoza, 2023; Malhotra et al., 2025), the transformative potential of accessible web platforms in reducing these barriers (Sundaramoorthy et al., 2024; Akanfe et al., 2025), and the importance of institutional support in enabling PWD entrepreneurship to move from aspiration to economic reality (Dakung et al., 2025; Csillag et al., 2026). Related systems demonstrate strong capabilities in e-commerce product management, transaction processing, and administrative oversight in various community producer contexts, from artisan marketplaces like Etsy and Krithika et al.'s (2026) women artisan platform to agricultural marketplaces developed for Filipino producers (Gadhve et al., 2025; Barayuga et al., 2025). Dominant methodological approaches across both literature and systems include qualitative analysis of barrier conditions, quantitative usability assessment, and system development methodologies following agile or iterative design principles.
Despite this wealth of related work, a consistent and critical gap emerges: no existing study or system provides a dedicated, web-accessible, institutionally co-managed e-commerce platform that positions PWDs in Philippine government rehabilitation centers as active, empowered sellers of their own livelihood products. Studies such as Gutterman (2025) and Fontana and Mitra (2023) articulate the market and policy case for PWD economic inclusion but offer no deployable technological solution. Systems such as Shopee, Lazada, and Amazon demonstrate comprehensive e-commerce capabilities but are entirely unsuited to PWD clients in AVRC's operational environment, lacking accessibility compliance, institutional oversight tools, and seller-side simplification. Local systems like BenguetFresh and AgriFresh demonstrate the viability of locally developed technology for Philippine government-supported livelihood programs but provide no marketplace functionality whatsoever. In the Philippine PWD context specifically, Mendoza (2023) documents the inadequacy of existing support systems but proposes only a conceptual framework rather than an implemented platform. No reviewed study or system establishes a direct operational link between a government vocational rehabilitation center's training outputs and a functional, accessible digital marketplace for product commercialization.
In direct response to these identified gaps, Inclusive Market introduces a novel and contextually grounded solution by developing a web-based smart e-commerce platform specifically designed for AVRC Region IX's PWD clients, institutional personnel, and LGU administrative staff. Its key differentiating features — TalkBack voice accessibility support and WCAG 2.1 compliance, PayMongo payment integration for locally accessible transactions, Google GunMailer authentication, LGU administrative monitoring and control, institution-specific product management, and a design philosophy centered on PWD empowerment rather than commercial optimization — collectively address the full spectrum of gaps identified across reviewed literature and systems. Inclusive Market does not seek to compete with or replicate large commercial platforms; rather, it creates an appropriate, accessible, and institutionally supported digital marketplace specifically calibrated to the operational realities of AVRC Region IX and the economic empowerment needs of its PWD clients. As such, it contributes a practical, replicable, and socially impactful model for advancing inclusive digital commerce and livelihood sustainability for persons with disabilities in the Philippines.

3.DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY
3.3 REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS
The requirements analysis phase involved comprehensive examination of the needs of all stakeholder groups — PWD clients as sellers, AVRC personnel and LGU staff as administrators, and general consumers as buyers. Data for requirements elicitation was gathered through direct observation of AVRC Region IX operations and selling activities, review of existing literature and related systems, and consultations with AVRC personnel and LGU staff. The analysis produced a structured set of functional and non-functional requirements guiding the system's design and development.
Stakeholder Identification
PWD Clients / Sellers. The sixty-six (66) plus enrolled PWD clients at AVRC Region IX who produce handmade crafts, handwoven products, decorative items, recycled goods, souvenir items, and gift articles. These users require a simplified, highly accessible interface with TalkBack support, enlarged interactive elements, clear instructional text, and minimal technical complexity to independently manage their product listings.
AVRC Personnel and LGU Staff / Administrators. Government staff responsible for overseeing platform operations, approving product listings, managing user accounts, monitoring inventory, processing transaction reports, and ensuring institutional accountability. These users require a comprehensive administrative dashboard with data visualization, management tools, and reporting capabilities.
General Consumers / Buyers. Members of the public in Region IX and beyond who will browse, search, and purchase PWD-made products through Inclusive Market. These users require an intuitive shopping experience with clear product information, reliable checkout processing, and accessible design.
Problem Identification
Absence of a centralized, accessible digital marketplace for consistent PWD product promotion and selling at AVRC Region IX.
Restricted market reach due to reliance on episodic physical selling events and informal social media activities.
Low product visibility due to absence of structured online promotion and organized product cataloging tools.
Digital accessibility barriers preventing independent platform use by PWD clients with sensory or motor impairments.
Absence of institutional administrative tools for AVRC and LGU staff to monitor, manage, and report on PWD selling activities.

3.3.5 Development Methodology
Inclusive Market is developed following the Agile Software Development Life Cycle with structured sprints ensuring iterative delivery, continuous stakeholder engagement, and systematic quality assurance. The six (6) project members collaborate across development, design, testing, and documentation roles throughout the following sprint schedule:
Sprint 1 (Weeks 1–2): Requirements Finalization and System Design. Requirements documentation finalization, database schema design, ERD and DFD development, UI wireframe and mockup creation, development environment setup, and technology stack configuration.
Sprint 2 (Weeks 3–4): Backend Core Development. Database implementation in MySQL/PostgreSQL, backend API development covering user management, Google GunMailer authentication integration, role-based access control, and product management CRUD operations.
Sprint 3 (Weeks 5–6): Frontend Development — Buyer and Seller Interfaces. Responsive web interface implementation for buyer-facing product catalog, search, cart, and checkout screens, and seller-facing product listing management screens. WCAG 2.1 compliance implementation and TalkBack ARIA labeling throughout.
Sprint 4 (Weeks 7–8): Payment Integration and Order Management. PayMongo Payment Gateway API integration, order placement and processing workflow development, inventory update automation, and order status notification system implementation.
Sprint 5 (Weeks 9–10): Administrative Dashboard and Reporting. LGU and AVRC administrator dashboard development including product approval workflow, user management tools, inventory monitoring interface, transaction oversight, and sales report generation.
Sprint 6 (Weeks 11–12): System Integration, Accessibility Testing, and UAT. Full system integration testing, WCAG 2.1 accessibility compliance verification, TalkBack functionality testing, User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with selected AVRC PWD clients and staff, and iterative refinement based on UAT feedback.
Sprint 7 (Week 13): Final Deployment, Evaluation, and Documentation. System deployment to web hosting environment, formal usability and accessibility evaluation with target respondents, evaluation instrument administration (SUS questionnaire and accessibility checklist), data analysis, and final documentation completion.

3.5 DESIGN OF SOFTWARE SYSTEM PRODUCTS AND PROCESS
Inclusive Market follows a three-tier client-server web application architecture consisting of the Presentation Layer (web frontend), the Application Logic Layer (backend server), and the Data Layer (relational database and integrated services). This layered architecture promotes separation of concerns, maintainability, and scalability appropriate for an institutional platform with multiple concurrent user types.
Presentation Layer. A responsive web application developed using HTML5, CSS3, and a modern JavaScript framework (such as Vue.js or React), providing accessible user interfaces for all three user roles — PWD sellers, administrators, and buyers. The frontend implements WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance through semantic HTML, ARIA labels, sufficient contrast ratios, and keyboard-navigable interactive elements. TalkBack compatibility is ensured through ARIA live regions, descriptive alt text, and logical focus management throughout all interface screens.
Application Logic Layer. A backend server built on a PHP/Laravel or Node.js/Express framework, handling business logic including role-based access control, product management workflows, order processing, PayMongo payment integration via RESTful API calls, Google GunMailer authentication via OAuth 2.0, inventory update logic, and administrative reporting generation. This layer communicates with the frontend via RESTful API endpoints and manages all data transactions with the database layer.
Data Layer. A MySQL or PostgreSQL relational database storing all system data including user profiles and roles, product listings and categories, inventory records, order records, transaction histories, payment records, product reviews, and system logs. The database schema is designed to support the complex relational queries required by the administrative dashboard and reporting modules while maintaining data integrity and compliance with RA 10173.
Integrated Services Layer. External service integrations include PayMongo Payment Gateway (payment processing via PayMongo API), Google GunMailer Authentication (OAuth 2.0 user authentication and email verification), and cloud-based file storage for product images.
